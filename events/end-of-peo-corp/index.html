A journal recovered by Agent Zomgoose after the END OF PEO CORP

DESCRIPTION: It was seemingly a typical day. We had just recently entered this new universe and established base underneath a pizzeria, closely guarded so that nothing unauthorized could enter. Our main room had a rather unorthodox hilltop, but it had all its necessary functions, so no one really objected.

In the days leading up to this event, we had already done a great deal of research, containing two local entities within base and setting up camp across the world; one notable camp was deep underwater near a trench.

That night, it began. The sound of something hitting the ground above us with incredible force prompted a reaction from anyone awake. I took a look outside with a fellow agent and saw that something foreign had landed. It was a massive robot that resembled a jellyfish, and the technology behind it was seemingly alien.

After lying dormant for a few seconds, it suddenly came to life, firing a beam at the nearby area. The beam decimated the surrounding grass, leaving a crater. We immediately got to work, attempting to engage with it as it was most definitely hostile.

Clashing with it was no easy task. After taking a few hits and recuperating, we discovered its weak spots to be its “head”; that is, the glass bulb on the very top. Focusing our firepower there allowed us to finally put it down, but at a cost, as the farmlands we had set up were erased by its beams.

We thought that was all, but suddenly, another large object landed near our area. And another. And another. And another. And another. And another.

46 hours of fighting had passed. Our resources were spent, but we still had enough to get our agents back on their feet. All of the invaders had fallen by now. We took the remains of some of them to research back in base. We were finally done fighting the onslaught.

Or so we thought. Nothing could have prepared me for this. None of the training I received when I first joined Peo Corp, none of the emergency manuals, none of the archived reports.

Nothing.

I have no idea when it arrived here. Maybe the initial assault was just a distraction. But I caught wind of it when I saw something in the distance. In the sky.

And it was getting closer.

I ran back down to the base, notifying the other agents immediately. Some didn’t believe me, being tired from the 46 hours of straight fighting.

Until everyone heard it.

Land being ripped up from the ground. Buildings crumbling in mere seconds. People, animals, and mobs running away. And for those unlucky enough to be caught in its gaze, only Peo knows what happened to them.

I was one of the first to evacuate via emergency protocol. That camp under the water proved to be a bastion of refuge while we decided our next move. Some of my fellow agents weren’t so lucky, as I never saw them on the other side.

We gave it an hour, and some of us, including myself, decided to return and see what happened. Our base was miraculously still intact, leading our captain to request all remaining agents to return to base.

I decided to take a look at the surface. It was all in ruins; the land itself looked like it was carved and grated, the grass all gone and ores exposed. The pizzeria was reduced to rubble, with only bits of its foundation left. Our base was partially exposed, though not broken. We somehow managed to survive.

An extravagant garden, basking in the sun. One that would put the Gardens of Babylon to shame. Glistening flowers covered bushes as far as the eye could see. But the most notable part of it was the fountain. What surrounded the fountain were clusters of vibrant, purple hyacinth. The type of flowers to glisten in the moonlight, as if they didn’t need the sun to grow.

I walked near these beauties. I came closer, taking in a waft of their wondrous scent. And as the sun started to set, I rested near them, seeking refuge, reminiscing, reflecting.

I noticed a pair of clippers on the ground, probably for the gardener to tend to the bushes, making sure they didn’t overgrow and disrupt other flowers.

The night became dark. One by one, the hyacinths were suddenly plucked from where they stood, being crumpled and then vanishing into the night sky. I desperately picked one for myself and grasped it within my two hands, shielding it. Holding it close to my heart.

Soft violet no longer circled the fountain. What remained on those bushes were.

They were. They were?

Black tentacles flailing in the sky, concealing the stars behind them. There was screaming as agents got caught in its gaze, trying to fight back but ultimately succumbing to their inevitable fate.

I ran into what remained of the base, barely getting away while the rest of it was sucked up by its gaze. Our captain had led us to our death. I regrouped at the camp with seven others, and after some time, we concluded we were the only survivors.

Days passed, and we made do on the food underwater as there was no way for us to return to North Peoville. Each of us would take turns scouting out how far it was from us, gauging how much time we had left.

One day, I decided to sail out a bit after completing my scouting routine. I stumbled upon untouched land. After venturing out for a bit and not finding any animals, I found something peculiar. A grey chest. Opening it required a code, but I broke the seal open with my tools.

Inside, I found an axe. A formidable one. It was called the “Constant Reminder”. The weight of it alone already told me how much power it contained. What’s it doing out here, in the middle of nowhere, in a chest locked by a passcode? Who put this here?

I sailed back and swam down to base, showing the other agents. They were confused, too, but agreed that it would be a big help.

The time had come. Our resources were scarce but enough to keep us afloat. It was above the ocean, getting closer. We were more prepared.

No one knew what happened when you got consumed by it. However, all of our attacks so far had done nothing to it. One of the agents spotted something, though. A small hole just above its heads. Perhaps it was mere luck or fate, but that hole only appeared to us now instead of earlier when numbers were still on our side.

We all voluntarily got stuck in its gaze, allowing us to get closer to the hole. Each of us took turns throwing ender pearls until we all successfully entered. Before we knew it, we found ourselves inside it.

Sun and moon would continue to pass, and only a cluster of hyacinths remained. Only the cluster, and the hyacinth in my own hands, glowed purple.

There was a full moon tonight, illuminating the fountain. The streams of water continued to flow down without end, unbreaking and continuous.

The clippers had reappeared, this time closer to my feet, as I continued to watch the fountain. A lone butterfly had landed on it, perhaps mistaking it for a flower. I kneeled near the clippers and let the butterfly crawl onto my hand.

And then I killed it. Or did I kill it? Why would I kill the butterfly, who has done nothing to me? Nothing to hurt me?

Because when I had come back to my senses, the clippers were in my hands. The butterfly was nowhere to be seen.

But the hyacinths were all gone. No more streaks of violet circled the fountain. The cluster had vanished. Only the hyacinth, still in my hands and crushed against the clippers, remained. Crumpled, and a shell of its former self, losing its vibrance.

I embedded the Constant Reminder into the beast’s heart, killing it. Ending this nightmare. Ending this hell.

And now, I have nothing left.

I dropped the clippers. And crushed the hyacinth in my hand, myself.
